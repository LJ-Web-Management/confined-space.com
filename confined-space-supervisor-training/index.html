Role & Purpose

Who Should Take This Course

🧑‍💼

Who Should Enroll

Entry supervisors, foremen, and site leads who authorize and cancel entry permits and take overall responsibility for a permit-required confined space entry.

🚫

Who Should Not Rely on This Course Alone

Entrants and attendants who don't authorize permits should take Permit-Required Entry training. Those who own the overall program need Competent Person training.

🏗️

Typical Job Titles

Entry supervisors, construction foremen, crew leads, field supervisors responsible for authorizing site-level permit entries.

Learning Objectives

What You'll Be Able to Do

  • Understand the entry supervisor's legal responsibilities under the standard
  • Evaluate site conditions and hazard controls before authorizing entry
  • Verify atmospheric monitoring results and equipment before signing a permit
  • Authorize, sign off on, and cancel entry permits correctly
  • Direct emergency response and reporting if entry conditions change
  • Coordinate with attendants, entrants, and the Competent Person during an entry

Regulatory Alignment

OSHA Standards Covered

This course is designed around the following OSHA standards. See the full OSHA Confined Space Standards guide for plain-language summaries and official links.

1926.1210: Entry Supervisors

Duties of entry supervisors authorizing and overseeing permit-required entries.

1926.1211: Rescue & Emergency

Rescue and emergency service coordination responsibilities.

29 CFR 1910.146

General Industry Permit-Required Confined Spaces standard, referenced where applicable.

Employer Responsibility

Online training can support OSHA compliance, but employers remain responsible for site-specific hazard evaluation, hands-on practice, rescue coordination, and confirming supervisors are qualified for assigned duties.

Some employers or worksites may require hands-on practice with atmospheric monitors, ventilation equipment, harnesses, retrieval systems, communication tools, permits, or rescue procedures. Online training should be paired with site-specific instruction where required by the employer, project owner, or applicable OSHA standard.
