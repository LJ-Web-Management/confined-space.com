Permit-Required Confined Space Training Guide

Every role that touches a permit-required confined space entry, and honestly, what training each one needs.

The Roles

Six Roles in a Permit-Required Entry

🧑‍🏭

Awareness-Level Worker

Works near, but never enters, confined spaces. Needs Awareness training for hazard recognition.

🧑‍🔧

Authorized Entrant

Physically enters the space. Needs Permit-Required Entry training, plus employer-provided hands-on PPE and monitor practice.

👁️

Attendant

Stays outside monitoring the entrant continuously and never enters. Also needs Permit-Required Entry training.

🧑‍💼

Entry Supervisor

Authorizes and cancels the entry permit. Needs Supervisor training.

🧭

Competent Person

Owns the site's overall confined space program. Needs Competent Person training, plus formal employer designation.

🚑

Rescue Personnel

Executes entry or non-entry rescue if something goes wrong. Rescue capability requires dedicated procedures, equipment, and practice beyond any single online course. See our rescue planning guide.

The Permit

What Has to Be On a Valid Entry Permit

  • Identification of the specific space
  • Purpose and duration of the entry
  • Hazards present and the measures used to isolate/control them
  • Acceptable entry conditions and atmospheric test results
  • Names of the entrant(s), attendant, and entry supervisor
  • Rescue and emergency procedures
  • Any additional permits in effect (e.g. hot work)

This guide summarizes OSHA's permit-required confined space framework for general understanding. It is not legal advice. See OSHA Confined Space Standards for official regulatory links, and confirm current requirements with qualified counsel or OSHA for your specific site.
