Regulatory Reference

OSHA Confined Space Standards Explained

Plain-language summaries of the standards our courses are aligned with, each linked to the official OSHA source. This page is general guidance, not legal advice.

Construction

29 CFR 1926 Subpart AA

Subpart AA Overview →

Confined Spaces in Construction. Requires identifying and classifying confined spaces on a construction site, a written permit program, training for every role, and rescue/emergency planning specific to construction work.

1926.1207: Training

Sets who must be trained, in what, and how retraining works when duties, hazards, or performance issues change. This is the section our Awareness and Entry courses are built around.

1926.1208: Duties of Authorized Entrants

What an entrant must know and do before, during, and after entering a permit space, covered in Permit-Required Entry training.

1926.1209: Duties of Attendants

The attendant's continuous monitoring, communication, and evacuation-authority duties, also covered in Permit-Required Entry training.

1926.1210: Duties of Entry Supervisors

Authorization, verification, and oversight duties for the entry supervisor, covered in Supervisor training.

1926.1211: Rescue & Emergency Services

Requirements for verified rescue capability before entry begins, covered in Supervisor and Competent Person training, and in our rescue planning guide.

General Industry

29 CFR 1910.146

1910.146 Overview →

Permit-Required Confined Spaces for General Industry. The general industry counterpart to 1926 Subpart AA, sharing the same core roles and permit concepts.

How It Differs From Subpart AA

1910.146 predates Subpart AA and doesn't include construction-specific requirements like continuous atmospheric monitoring or multi-employer information exchange that construction sites need.

State Rules

State Plans May Differ

Roughly half the states run their own OSHA-approved State Plan, which must be at least as protective as federal OSHA and sometimes adds requirements. See our State OSHA Plan Requirements guide or OSHA's official State Plans directory →.
